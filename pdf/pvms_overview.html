■What you are looking at

A working prototype of the Preferred Vendor Management System, built on your NetSuite account rather than alongside it. Three screens cover the whole vendor lifecycle: a vendor applies, the Port reviews and approves, and the vendor then manages their own compliance from a portal. Every screen is live and clickable today.

Today vendor onboarding at the Port runs on email, spreadsheets and PDF attachments. Applications arrive incomplete, certificates of insurance expire without anyone noticing until a purchase order is held, and there is no single place to see where a vendor is in the process. PVMS closes that loop inside the system you already run your purchasing in.

■The three components

01Intake

New Vendor Application

The form a prospective vendor fills in, and the first place the Port either saves or loses time. It asks for company details, ownership, contacts, insurance, W-9 and the NIGP commodity codes the vendor wants to be considered for, in a defined order with the rules applied as they type. A vendor cannot submit an application that is missing a required certificate or an expired COI, so the incomplete submission that today generates three rounds of email simply never arrives.

  • Company, ownership, contact and compliance capture in a guided sequence
  • Insurance certificate, W-9 and small or disadvantaged business certification upload
  • NIGP commodity code selection, so the vendor is searchable from day one
  • Validation at the point of entry rather than at the point of review
02Vendor

Vendor Portal

Where an approved vendor lives for the rest of the relationship. They see where their application stands, which of their documents are current and which are about to lapse, their open purchase orders, their submitted bills and what the Port has paid. When a certificate is close to expiry the vendor is prompted and replaces it themselves. That single behaviour removes most of the chasing your team does today, because the party who holds the document is the party being asked for it.

  • Application status and full approval history, visible to the vendor
  • Document library with expiry dates and advance renewal prompts
  • Purchase orders, bills and payment history drawn from NetSuite itself
  • Self service updates to contacts, addresses and banking detail
03Port staff

PVMS inside NetSuite

The internal workspace, and the reason this is a NetSuite project rather than a portal project. Purchasing works a review queue that shows how long each application has been waiting and who it is with. Risk and IT security approve in sequence, each step recorded. On final approval the vendor record is created in NetSuite directly, with the commodity codes, the documents and the approval trail attached to it. Compliance reporting is then a query against records you already hold, not a spreadsheet somebody assembles.

  • Review queue with status, ageing and overdue flags
  • Multi step approval workflow with an owner and a timestamp at each step
  • Certificate of insurance tracking with expiry alerts before a PO is affected
  • Compliance dashboards and a complete audit trail

■How a vendor moves through it

Step 1
Apply
The vendor completes the guided application and uploads their documents. The form will not let them submit something incomplete, so what reaches the Port is ready to be reviewed.
Step 2
Review
It appears in the Purchasing queue with every document attached. Risk and IT security review in turn, and anyone can see whose desk it is on and how long it has been there.
Step 3
Approve
Approval creates the vendor record in NetSuite directly. No rekeying, no second system.
Step 4
Maintain
The vendor keeps their own documents current from the portal and is prompted before anything lapses. The Port is alerted too, well before an expiry can hold up a purchase order.

■Built on NetSuite, not beside it

PVMS is native to your NetSuite account. That is a deliberate choice and it is what separates this from a bolt-on vendor portal. The vendor record, the purchase orders, the bills and the payment history are the same records your finance team already works in. There is no synchronisation to break, no second source of truth to reconcile, and no additional platform licence.

The prototype in front of you is the user experience, decided before any build begins. What you change now costs nothing to change.
